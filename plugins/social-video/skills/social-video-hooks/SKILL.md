# Short-Form Social Video: Hooks & Retention Instructions

Write instructions that a creator or editor can execute without asking follow-up questions. Every beat gets a timestamp, a spoken line, and an on-screen/camera action.

## The core model

Short-form retention is not one hook at the front. It is **stacked hooks at t=0, then a new retention device every ~3 seconds** for the life of the clip.

Two rules drive everything below:

1. **Stack at zero.** At least three devices fire simultaneously in the first second — a visual, a verbal, and a title. They compete for different attention channels, so they add rather than overlap.
2. **Never coast.** The gap between retention devices should not exceed ~3–4 seconds. A viewer who goes 5 seconds with no new stimulus scrolls.

## The six devices

| Device | Channel | How to execute |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual hook** | Motion | Slow, continuous push-in (zoom) starting frame 1. Movement must be perceptible but not distracting. |
| **Verbal hook** | Audio | Open on a *question*, not a statement. The question should be answerable "yes" by the target viewer. |
| **Title hook** | Text (upper) | Text card above the head, present from frame 1. States the payoff, not the topic. |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Behavior | Do something physically unexpected mid-sentence — an unrelated action that breaks the talking-head frame. |
| **Text hook** | Text (lower) | Caption/keyword under the chin, mid-clip. Reinforces the spoken line; does not duplicate it verbatim. |
| **Rehook** | Framing | Hard cut to a new camera angle or focal length. Resets attention without changing content. |

**Placement:** visual + verbal + title at 0:00. Pattern interrupt, text hook, and rehook are spent across the body of the clip, spaced to hold the ~3s cadence.

## Output format

When asked for a brief, script, or instructions, produce a **beat sheet** — not prose:

```
HOOK STACK (0:00)
  Visual  — [camera move, starting frame 1]
  Verbal  — "[exact opening question]"
  Title   — "[exact on-screen text, above head]"

BEAT 2 (0:0X) — [device]
  Say    — "[exact line]"
  Do     — [action / camera / text]

BEAT 3 (0:0X) — [device]
  ...

CTA (0:XX)
  Say    — "[exact closing line]"
  Do     — [end card / gesture]
```

Rules for the beat sheet:
- **Write the exact words**, never "introduce the topic" or "explain the benefit." The creator should be able to read it aloud.
- **Timestamp every beat.** If two consecutive beats are >4s apart, insert a device.
- **Name the device** on each beat so the creator understands why it exists.
- Keep total runtime tight — 20–45s unless told otherwise.

## Self-demonstration

The strongest instructional format for this content is a clip that **performs each technique at the moment it names it** — the camera zooms while the line says "this is a visual hook." Reach for this when the video's subject *is* the technique. It collapses explanation and proof into the same second.

## Checklist before delivering

- [ ] Three devices stacked at 0:00
- [ ] No gap >4s without a new device
- [ ] Opening line is a question
- [ ] Title text states the payoff, not the topic
- [ ] Every beat has exact spoken words
- [ ] Pattern interrupt is genuinely unexpected, not just a gesture
- [ ] CTA is a single clear action

## Anti-patterns

- Leading with context or a greeting — the question comes first, always.
- Title text that restates the caption or the spoken line word-for-word.
- Saving all the hooks for the front and coasting through the back half.
- Vague direction ("be energetic", "talk about the benefits") — unexecutable.
- A pattern interrupt that reads as a nervous tic rather than a deliberate break.
